How to Calculate Discounts and Sale Prices

A discount percentage tells you how much is removed from an original price. This guide explains the discount amount, final sale price and the difference between one discount and several discounts.

Calculate the discount amount

Convert the discount rate to a decimal and multiply it by the original price.

Discount amount = original price × discount rate

For a ₹2,000 item with a 15% discount, the discount is ₹2,000 × 0.15 = ₹300.

Calculate the final sale price

Subtract the discount amount from the original price, or multiply the original price by one minus the discount rate.

Sale price = original price × (1 − discount rate)

Using the same example, ₹2,000 × 0.85 = ₹1,700.

What happens with tax?

A discount and a tax are separate operations. A store may apply a discount before tax, after tax, or under a specific promotion's terms. If tax is applied after the discount, calculate the discounted price first and then apply the applicable tax rate.

Two discounts are not simply added

Suppose an item receives 20% off followed by another 10% off. The remaining fraction is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, so the combined reduction is 28%, not 30%. The order can matter when different rules or taxes are involved.

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting the percentage number directly instead of converting it to a rate.
  • Using the discounted price as the original price for a second calculation without checking the promotion rules.
  • Assuming a displayed percentage includes taxes, shipping or other fees.
  • Rounding each intermediate amount when the final invoice uses more precise values.

Check the result

The discount amount should be zero at 0% and equal to the full original price at 100%. For rates between those limits, the sale price should stay between zero and the original price when no other charges are included.

Use the calculator

Use the Discount Calculator to calculate the savings and sale price quickly. The Percentage Calculator can help with general percentage questions.

Important limitation

Retail prices can include taxes, fees, coupons, shipping and store-specific rules that a simple discount calculation does not model. Check the seller's final price before purchasing.
