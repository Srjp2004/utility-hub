How Loan Payments Are Calculated: Principal, Interest and Term

A standard loan payment depends mainly on the amount borrowed, periodic interest rate and number of payments. Understanding those inputs helps you interpret a calculator result.

The three core inputs

Principal is the amount borrowed. Interest rate is the lender's stated rate, which must be converted to the periodic rate used by the calculation. Term is the number of payment periods.

Standard monthly payment formula

For a standard fixed-rate amortizing loan, the periodic payment can be calculated as P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is principal, r is the periodic interest rate and n is the number of payments.

If the rate is zero, the payment is simply principal divided by the number of payments.

Worked example

For a hypothetical ₹120,000 loan at 6% annual interest over 24 monthly payments, the monthly rate is 0.06 ÷ 12. The calculator applies that periodic rate across the 24 payments and can report the resulting monthly payment, total repayment and total interest.

Why term changes the result

A longer term generally spreads repayment over more periods. That can reduce the periodic payment while increasing the amount of interest paid over the life of the loan, assuming the rate and other conditions remain comparable.

Costs a basic formula may not include

  • Origination or processing fees.
  • Insurance, taxes or other required charges.
  • Variable or promotional interest rates.
  • Late fees, prepayments or irregular payment schedules.
  • Changes in the payment frequency or compounding convention.

Common input mistakes

  • Entering an annual rate as though it were already a monthly rate.
  • Confusing years with the total number of monthly payments.
  • Comparing loans with different fees using payment amount alone.
  • Relying on a rounded estimate instead of the lender's actual repayment schedule.

Important limitation

This is an educational calculation, not a lending offer or financial advice. Actual loan agreements can use different conventions and charges. Verify consequential financial decisions against the lender's documents and applicable official information.
